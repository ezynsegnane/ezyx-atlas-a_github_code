--- Contribution and scope ---

The manuscript presents a controlled measurement study of multimodal ECG
classification. Its central mathematical contributions are: (i) a formal multi-label
problem setting with an explicit missingness-aware feature representation (Section 3);
(ii) an availability-quality score q_meta derived from the metadata availability mask,
used as a continuous attenuator in a zero-initialised residual injection and a
GLU-style multiplicative fusion gate (Sections 4.2--4.5); (iii) a composite multi-task
loss that combines weighted binary cross-entropy with an unsquared pairwise AUC
surrogate (Section 5.1); and (iv) a rigorous paired statistical protocol using exact
two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Benjamini--Hochberg FDR correction across a
pre-declared 3-test confirmatory family (the three pairwise macro-AUC contrasts of
Group A), Cohen d_z and Hedges g_z effect sizes, and 10,000-resample percentile
bootstrap confidence intervals (Section 5.5).

The study is aligned with the scope of Mathematics through its formal mathematical
framework and its use of nonparametric statistical inference to answer a concrete
scientific question: whether, and for which ECG pathology classes, structured clinical
metadata adds statistically measurable information beyond a strong ECG-only baseline.

The main finding is that both metadata conditions yield statistically significant
gains once 20 paired seeds are used. Demographic variables alone (age, sex) yield a
small but significant gain (Delta = +0.0007 macro-AUC, 95% paired CI [0.0002, 0.0011],
BH-adjusted p ≈ 0.009), localised in the NORM and MI superclasses. Full metadata
(demographics + anthropometrics) produce a larger and highly significant gain
(Delta = +0.0019 macro-AUC, 95% paired CI [0.0014, 0.0023], BH-adjusted p < 0.001),
concentrated in NORM, MI, and STTC. CD and HYP show no statistically significant
effect in either contrast. Critically, the small demographic-only gain was NOT
detectable at 10 seeds (the pilot campaign), illustrating the value of the 20-seed
paired protocol introduced here.

--- Novelty and reproducibility ---

This paper does not propose a state-of-the-art ECG architecture. Its novelty is the
protocol: a seed-matched three-variant ablation (none / demo / demo+anthro) with
paired multi-seed statistics, per-class decomposition, and an inference-time
anthropometric masking stress test, on the standard PTB-XL 1.0.3 benchmark. To our
knowledge no prior PTB-XL study separates demographic from anthropometric metadata
under a paired multi-seed protocol with explicit missingness stress testing.

The campaign comprises 250 unique runs organised in six experimental groups (A--F)
across 270 seed-variant descriptors. The primary confirmatory Group A (3 variants x
20 seeds) is accompanied by exploratory groups covering fusion-capacity sensitivity
(Group B), loss-weight sensitivity (Group C), augmentation ablation (Group D),
architectural ablations including ECG-only and meta-only controls (Group E), and
multi-split generalisation (Group F). All 250 runs were executed on CPU-only hardware
(Intel Core i5, 8 GB RAM, PyTorch 2.3.1+cpu) without GPU or CUDA, making the results
free of CUDA non-determinism and straightforward to replicate.

--- Supplementary package ---

The reviewed submission is accompanied by a self-contained supplementary package that
includes: all 250 seed-level result JSON files across six groups; derived CSV and
Markdown statistical tables; paired comparison reports with bootstrap confidence
intervals; a dataset-integrity report; a pre-specification document (analysis_plan.md,
whose SHA-256 is embedded in every result JSON to document confirmatory intent);
environment files (requirements.txt and environment.yml); the complete training,
evaluation, and figure-generation source code; and the six final manuscript figures.
Patient-level probability arrays were not archived; this is documented explicitly in
the manuscript data-availability statement.

--- Ethics and data ---

This study uses only the public, fully de-identified PTB-XL v1.0.3 dataset available
on PhysioNet (doi:10.13026/kfzx-aw45). No patient-level data are included in the
submission package. Ethical review was waived in accordance with the PTB-XL original
approval. No new clinical data collection was performed.
